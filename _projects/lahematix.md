## The challenge

Classroom research asks teachers to connect an educational idea with what actually happens in a lesson. Plans, teaching materials, observations and reflections often live in separate places. Teachers need a manageable way to organise that work and share what others can reuse.

## The response

LAHEMATIX is the research platform for LAHEMATE, a programme investigating learner-centred mathematical problem solving. It gives teacher-researchers and researchers a shared setting for developing and sharing lesson plans. Its public interface provides a searchable lesson gallery and a task bank; project participation is accessed through a separate sign-in.

## My role

I am principal investigator of the LAHEMATE project, as recorded in ETIS. That research leadership is distinct from individual software implementation contributions, which are not attributed here without confirmation.

## Research basis

LAHEMATE brings together approaches associated with Jo Boaler's Mathematical Mindset, Peter Liljedahl's Thinking Classroom, and Toh and colleagues' Mathematical Problem Solving for Everyone. The project adapts and investigates these approaches in Estonian schools and teacher education.

The wider research logic is iterative: develop an intervention, try it in an authentic classroom, examine evidence, and refine the design. Classroom inquiry and teacher-as-researcher practice connect directly with my work in mathematics teacher education.

## Design and implementation

The public workflow makes existing lesson designs discoverable before asking visitors to create an account.

1. **Find a teaching approach.** Browse the gallery by age group, method and lesson type.
2. **Inspect a lesson plan.** Review its topic, author, duration and approach.
3. **Adapt and investigate.** Use the shared material as a starting point for classroom inquiry; project members continue through the authenticated research environment.
4. **Share a reusable design.** Public gallery materials are provided under CC BY 4.0, with attribution to their authors.

## Evidence and impact

The live platform exposes lesson plans and teaching resources. This is evidence of a working public resource, not proof of improved learning outcomes. The research programme runs through September 2027; evaluation findings will be added when they are available. Planned participation targets are not reported as achieved impact.
